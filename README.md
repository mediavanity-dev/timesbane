# timesbane

A wordless, mobile-first artwork at **https://timesbane.com**. It works on a computer too. It doesn't explain itself, so this file does. **Everything below is a spoiler.**

What's still planned is in [BACKLOG.md](BACKLOG.md).

## Opening

A black screen with one breathing dot. If nothing happens for a few seconds, faint rings pulse out from it. Tap or click it (or press Enter) to begin. That first gesture also turns on sound and, on iPhone, asks for motion access.

The opening also reflects your history:
- Faint dots circle the dot, one for each previous visit.
- Ember points circle closer in, one for each movement you've never seen. They glow brighter right after the site has been updated with something new.
- At a mirror minute (1:11, 2:22, 10:10, 11:11, 12:12…) the dot is two.
- On your 10th, 50th, 100th, 250th, 500th and 1000th visits, a gold ring surrounds it.
- If you come at an hour you've never visited before (after five visits), the dot is pale blue.

## The sixteen movements

They drift into one another on their own. Add `#m0` to `#m15` to the URL to open one directly.

| # | Movement | Idea | What happens and what you can do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Shy | Looking at something changes it | A flowing swarm of light. Touch and everything freezes, desaturates and goes quiet, and you hear a heartbeat (with a vibration on Android). Let go and the swarm rushes to where your finger was. Leave it alone and hold the phone still, and it slowly forms an eye that blinks and looks at you, or at where you were, or at the ghost of your past gestures. A shake scatters it |
| 1 | Unbreaking | Time running backwards | Paper covered in black shards. Tap and the shards fly back together (accelerating, as in a rewound film) into a vessel with gold kintsugi seams, which rings like a bell. After a few seconds it breaks again; tap a whole vessel to break it sooner. Drag to sweep debris, tilt to slide it, shake to shatter everything. Vessel shapes are new each visit |
| 2 | The Cave | Dimensions we can't see | A tesseract, a 24-cell or a Clifford torus turning in four dimensions, coloured by its depth in the fourth. You hear each vertex as it crosses into our 3D. Drag (or scroll) to rotate in 4D, tap to make it glow, double-tap for the next shape, tilt or shake to spin it. A small figure on the floor watches its shadow |
| 3 | Asemic | Meaning comes from the reader | An invisible hand writes in a script that isn't a language (a new alphabet every visit). Every few words a real word appears and soon fades; across many words they form sentences ("nobody is writing this", "meaning arrives from you"…) in your language. Drag to smudge the wet ink, tap to move the pen |
| 4 | Droste | Self-reference | A rainbow tunnel of phone screens inside phone screens. Draw into it and your lines spiral down, with a voice that echoes (the sound repeats itself, like the picture). Hold still and the tunnel reverses, pouring screens out toward you. Tilt bends it, scrolling pushes it in or out, shake bursts it; speed and spin are new each visit |
| 5 | Absence | Things grow when you aren't looking | A tree under the real sky. Hold your finger down and darkness spreads (eyes closed); in the dark the tree grows fast, with a rising melody. Leave the tab and it grows while you're gone. Once in bloom, petals fall, the tree dies and a new seed sprouts. Tilt is wind, shake knocks the blossoms off. One star for each of your visits; the newest one pulses |
| 6 | Sunflower | One irrational number | Seeds spiral out at the golden angle, 137.5°. Your finger (or the scroll wheel) bends the angle by fractions of a degree and the flower collapses into spokes, each count playing a note. Let go and it heals with a bell |
| 7 | Three Bodies | Order is fragile | Three suns on the real figure-eight orbit (Chenciner–Montgomery), playing a repeating rhythm. Tap to add a star, flick to throw one, and the order breaks into chaos. Left alone, a visitor eventually arrives: today's real closest asteroid, with its real speed and size. Escaping suns leave with a low note. Double-tap restores the figure-eight; shake knocks them off course |
| 8 | Butterfly | Sensitive dependence (Lorenz attractor) | 420 points start one ten-millionth apart and move as a single dot for about 25 seconds, then smear across the whole butterfly. Each wing switch is a note, so one note becomes a rain of notes. Tap to collapse the cloud again from that point, drag or scroll to turn it, shake to split it at once |
| 9 | Chladni | Sound has shape | Sand on a vibrating plate jumps off the moving parts and settles on the still lines, forming geometric figures. Touch, drag or scroll across the plate to change the note; left alone, it changes every few seconds. Shake throws the sand. Thunder where you are strikes a new note; a large earthquake anywhere on Earth today makes the plate tremble by itself |
| 10 | Epicycles | Anything is circles (Fourier series) | A shape (one of your own past strokes, or an invented one) is redrawn by circles turning on circles, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8… of them as the cycles pass. The circles also sound as a chord. Draw anything and it becomes the new shape |
| 11 | Relativity | Time is not the same everywhere | A grid of ticking clocks. Near your finger, or a slow invisible mass that wanders past, they slow down, turn red and bend inward. Afterwards they stay out of sync, so the single tick becomes a scatter of clicks. Double-tap to resynchronise; shake scrambles them. Between 3 and 4 a.m. they run backwards |
| 12 | Murmur | Humanity writing | Every new page created on any Wikipedia (or other Wikimedia site), live, as a point of light: the angle is the language, the distance is the size, bots are faint and blue. Pages in the same language are joined by threads. Hold still and the field holds its breath |
| 13 | Today | One rule, one hour, everyone | A pattern grown from a single cellular-automaton rule, chosen and seeded by the current hour (UTC). Everyone on Earth looking at it sees the same pattern at the same moment. Tap a cell and your version diverges: your changes spread down the page in ember, yours alone |
| 14 | Pendulums | Your gravity, the real minute | Eighteen pendulums whose lengths are set by the actual strength of gravity at your latitude. They are released together at the top of every real minute, drift into waves and patterns, and line up again at the next minute, for everyone at once. Drag across them to strum |
| 15 | Skin | Patterns from two chemicals (Gray–Scott reaction-diffusion) | The same equations that paint spots, stripes, coral and mazes on animals. Today's pattern type is set by the date (and nudged by the temperature where you are). Paint with your finger to seed it; shake scatters seeds |

## Getting around

- **Wait:** each movement moves on by itself after about a minute without input. Movements you haven't seen yet come first, so anything new added to the site finds you first.
- **The turning mark:** tap the slowly turning mark in the bottom-right corner.
- **Two-finger swipe:** left goes forward; right goes back through where you've been.
- **The corner door:** press and hold the top-left corner. A ring slowly closes, then a door opens to a random movement.
- **Face-down:** turn the phone face-down, then back up, and you wake somewhere else.
- **Sound:** the mark in the top-right corner turns sound on and off.

## On a computer

The site detects a mouse and adapts. Sound works the same way (it starts with your first click or key press).

| Input | Does |
|---|---|
| Click, drag | Same as tap and drag |
| Move the mouse | Stands in for tilt (the Cave, Droste, Absence); Shy's eye follows it |
| Shake the mouse rapidly side to side | Same as shaking the phone |
| Scroll wheel | Rotates the Cave, pushes Droste's tunnel, bends the Sunflower, turns the Butterfly, steps Chladni's note |
| Right-click | Same as double-tap |
| Hold the space bar | A held finger wherever the mouse is (freeze Shy, grow the tree in the dark, reverse Droste…) |
| Hold Shift while pressing in Relativity | An event horizon (the three-finger secret) |
| → and ← | Next movement, and back |
| R | A random movement (the face-down flip) |
| S | Shake |
| M | Sound on/off |
| F | Fullscreen |

The cursor disappears after a few seconds without movement.

## Sensors and inputs

- **Touch:** tap, hold, drag, flick, double-tap and multi-touch, as described above.
- **Tilt** (orientation): slides the debris in Unbreaking, turns the Cave's 4D object, bends Droste's tunnel, blows the wind in Absence and nudges the Sunflower's angle.
- **Shake** (accelerometer): scatters Shy's swarm, shatters every vessel, flings the 4D object, bursts the Droste tunnel, shakes the blossoms off the tree, knocks the three suns off course, splits the Butterfly cloud, throws the Chladni sand, scrambles the clocks and scatters Skin's seeds.
- **Stillness:** Shy's eye only forms while the phone is held still.
- **Compass:** where the phone reports a heading, the sky in Absence turns as you turn.
- **Vibration:** Android only.
- **Battery:** below 15% and unplugged, the piece is tired (Chrome/Android).
- **Leaving the tab:** Absence keeps growing, and the tab title changes (below).

iPhone asks for motion access on the first tap.

## Memory: never the same twice

Everything it remembers is kept in this browser's `localStorage`.

- **Your ghost:** your past gestures replay as a ghost. Shy's swarm freezes where the ghost "touches", and the eye sometimes watches it instead of you. Droste draws the ghost's strokes into the tunnel, and Epicycles redraws your old strokes as circles.
- **Each visit is new:** a new alphabet, flow field (seeded by real atmospheric noise from random.org when available), eye shape, set of vessel shapes, tunnel, starting 4D view and tree.
- **Never the same place:** it remembers which movements you've seen and which hours you visit at.
- **It ages with you:** one dot at the opening and one star in the sky per visit, a larger tree each time you come back, and a golden tree on the anniversary of your first visit.

### Updates and offline

- **A copy for offline:** a service worker keeps a copy of the piece in the browser, so it opens offline (live data simply sleeps).
- **Updates arrive straight away:** the page is always fetched fresh when you're online, so updates reach you the moment they're published.
- **Your history carries over:** your ghost, stars, gold stars and seen movements are kept through every update, and older saved data is migrated automatically.
- **New things find you first:** when new movements are added, a returning visitor sees them as brighter ember points at the opening, and the order steers them to the new movements first.

## The world it lives in

It knows *when* you are, roughly *where* you are, and a little of what is happening on Earth right now. Every source is optional: if it's missing or offline, nothing breaks.

### Sky, sun and moon

- **The real sky in Absence:**
  - the 53 brightest stars, with faint lines for Orion, the Plough, Cassiopeia, the Summer Triangle and the Southern Cross;
  - the Moon at its real position and phase;
  - the sky colour following the real Sun through night, twilight and day.
- **Golden hour and blue hour** tint every movement around the real sunrise and sunset.
- **True noon:** for a few minutes at solar noon, the Cave's object casts no shadow.
- **The other side of the world:** when it's deep night for you, a faint glow under the hill marks the daylight on the far side of the Earth.

### The calendar

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| Solstices and equinoxes | The longest day has a little more light all day, the shortest a little more night. On the equinox, the left half of Relativity's clocks turns night-blue and the right half day-warm |
| Meteor showers (Quadrantids, Lyrids, Eta Aquariids, Perseids, Orionids, Leonids, Geminids) | Far more falling stars in Absence |
| Solar eclipse days | The moon passes over Shy's eye |
| Lunar eclipse days | A blood-red moon in Absence |
| Full and new moon | Asemic's hidden sentence is about tonight's moon |
| 29 February | Everything is mirrored, a day that shouldn't exist |
| Pi day (14 March) | The Sunflower turns by 1/π instead of the golden ratio; Epicycles draws π |
| Fibonacci day (23 November) | The Sunflower's spirals turn gold |
| Friday the 13th | Vessels break almost as soon as they're whole |
| The day of the week | Sunday and Monday are slower, Friday and Saturday faster |

### Holidays: yours, where you live

Your country comes from your timezone (or your browser's region), and your state or province where the timezone shows it. Public holidays come from the Nager.Date service, including regional ones (for example a holiday only in New York, Ontario or New South Wales). On a holiday the whole piece takes on a theme:

| Holiday kind | Theme |
|---|---|
| New Year, independence and national days | Fireworks |
| Christmas | Snow |
| Easter and spring feasts | Pastel petals |
| Thanksgiving, harvest and mid-autumn | Falling amber leaves |
| Memorial, Remembrance, Veterans, Anzac Day | Falling poppies, and two minutes of silence at 11:00 |
| Lunar New Year | Red and gold lanterns rising |
| Diwali | A row of lamps |
| Eid | Lanterns and a crescent moon |
| Holi | Clouds of coloured powder |
| Hanukkah | One more candle each night, with the shamash |
| Día de Muertos, All Souls | Marigold petals |
| St Patrick's Day | Green |

Some festivals are also recognised without the network, from built-in date tables (through 2030), where they're widely observed: Lunar New Year, Diwali, Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha, Holi, Hanukkah, Halloween and Día de Muertos. Lunar dates can differ by a day locally. The piece's accent colours and Absence's blossoms take the holiday's colours.

### Time of day

- **The dead of night (3–4 a.m.):** everything slows, and Relativity's clocks run backwards.
- **Dawn chorus:** around sunrise, birdsong is synthesised into every movement.
- **Commutes:** weekday rush hours run slightly faster.
- **Long sessions:** after 20 minutes, the piece grows drowsy and dim.
- **The end of each hour:** in the last ten seconds, soft rising ticks, then a bell on the hour (Relativity strikes the hour itself).
- **Exactly midnight:** for one minute, everything is inside out.

### Weather where you are (Open-Meteo)

- **Rain and snow** fall over every movement, and Asemic's ink runs in the rain.
- **Thunderstorms** bring lightning, thunder and a new note on the Chladni plate.
- **Fog** softens everything.
- **Wind** speeds the tree's sway and Droste's spin.
- **Real cloud cover** hides the real stars.
- **Temperature** tints the palette (cold blue below 0 °C, warm above 28 °C) and nudges Skin's pattern.

### Air, sea and life near you

- **Air pollution (PM2.5):** haze on polluted days.
- **Pollen:** drifting grains in the Sunflower.
- **The sea:** near the coast, Absence shows the real sea at the real tide height and wave size.
- **Altitude:** high up (above 1500 m), the stars are brighter.
- **Fifty years ago:** Absence shows a faint ghost tree for the same day in that year, larger if it was colder then and tinted by the difference.
- **Local species:** the number of species people have recorded nearby this month on iNaturalist sets how richly the tree branches.

### The planet and beyond

- **The International Space Station:** when it's actually above you, it crosses the Absence sky as a steady light.
- **Aurora:** at high latitudes, or during a strong geomagnetic storm (NOAA Kp index), curtains of green and violet light.
- **Solar flares:** after M- or X-class flares (NOAA GOES), the Cave's object flares.
- **Earthquakes:** a magnitude 6+ quake anywhere today (USGS) makes the Chladni plate tremble.
- **Today's closest asteroid** (NASA NeoWs) is the Three Bodies visitor.
- **The Arctic:** a white floe in Unbreaking, as large as the real sea ice is now compared with the same month in 1979.
- **Bitcoin:** each new block on the chain (mempool.space) makes a vessel whole.
- **A painting's colour:** Droste's first colour comes from a painting made exactly a hundred years ago (Art Institute of Chicago).
- **Wikipedia:** Murmur is every new Wikipedia page, live.
- **UK only:** when the electricity grid is carbon-heavy (carbonintensity.org.uk), the piece is a little dimmer.

### Language and seasons

- **Language:** Asemic's real words are in your browser language (English, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese or Italian).
- **Seasons:** the tree carries frost, blossom, leaves or turning leaves for your season and hemisphere.
- **Hemisphere:** the Sunflower turns the other way in the south, and stands still on the equator.

## Privacy

- **What every page can read:** the clock, the timezone and the browser language. From these it guesses your city.
- **Location is opt-in only:** double-tap in Absence and the browser asks. If you allow it, the sky and the tree snap to your exact place. If you walk more than 200 m while Absence is open, the tree lets its blossoms go (the position is watched only while you're there, then released).
- **What leaves your device:** your place, rounded to about 10 km (the timezone city, or your opted-in position), is sent only to Open-Meteo (weather, air, sea, elevation, history) and iNaturalist (species counts). All other sources are global and get no location at all. Exact coordinates are never stored or sent.
- **Cached in this browser:** answers are kept in `localStorage` for minutes to days, so it asks rarely. They contain the data, never your coordinates.
- **Data saver:** the Murmur stream only runs while that movement is on screen, and not at all if your browser asks to save data.
- **Analytics:** the site also carries the shared Google Analytics tag, which records ordinary page visits.

## Sound

Everything is synthesised live with Web Audio: a drone in a different key for each movement, reverb and an echo line, plus birdsong at dawn and thunder in storms. Nearly every sound is the maths or the data itself, not a soundtrack:
- vertices crossing into the 4th dimension (the Cave);
- Lorenz wing switches (Butterfly);
- orbital turning points (Three Bodies);
- spoke counts (Sunflower);
- Fourier coefficients as a chord (Epicycles);
- clock ticks (Relativity);
- new Wikipedia pages (Murmur);
- the automaton's density (Today);
- pendulums crossing the middle (Pendulums).

## Tech

- **The page:** `index.html` (Canvas 2D and Web Audio, no dependencies, no build step) plus `sw.js` (the service worker). Only the Cormorant Garamond font loads from Google Fonts.
- **Live data:** Open-Meteo, NOAA SWPC, USGS, wheretheiss.at, NASA NeoWs (demo key), iNaturalist, global-warming.org, carbonintensity.org.uk, Wikimedia EventStreams, mempool.space, Nager.Date, the Art Institute of Chicago and random.org. All are free, called directly from the browser and cached.
- **Hosting:** GitHub Pages from `main`, with the custom domain set in `CNAME`.
- **DNS:** GoDaddy DNS for `timesbane.com` has four `A` records on `@` pointing to 185.199.108.153, 185.199.109.153, 185.199.110.153 and 185.199.111.153, plus a `CNAME` on `www` pointing to `mediavanity-dev.github.io`.
- **Dashboard:** the site appears on the repo dashboard at https://mediavanity-dev.github.io/dashboard/.
